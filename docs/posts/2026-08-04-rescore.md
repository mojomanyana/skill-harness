# Changing your pass threshold shouldn't cost another $40 of tokens

You ran 18 scenarios × 5 reps across three models. Then you decided that
"passes 3 of 5 reps" is too generous for a critical scenario and the bar should
be 4 of 5.

The obvious move is to re-run everything. That's wrong, and it's expensive.

Reps are a **measurement**: this scenario passed 3 of 5 clean attempts. The
threshold is **policy**: how many passes I'm willing to call a pass. Changing
policy does not invalidate a measurement — and re-running to accommodate a
policy change actually makes things worse, because you come back with *different*
measurements and can no longer tell which change moved the number.

```bash
skill-harness rescore <run-dir>
```

No model calls. No judge calls. It re-collapses the reps you already have under
the current spec's thresholds and prints exactly what moved:

```
  A1: PASS → FAIL  (3/5 @ threshold 0.8)
  → C (70%) 7/10 NOT READY
```

Overrides, notes, and suspect flags survive untouched — this changes the collapse
rule, not anything about what the judge said or what you concluded.

## The same principle, one level up

`rescore` doesn't touch the judge. Its sibling does:

```bash
skill-harness grade <run-dir> --judge <a-different-model>
```

That re-grades the **saved transcripts** with a different judge — still no agent
re-runs, because the transcripts are the expensive artifact and they're already on
disk. Suspect a judge is being generous? De-confound it with a second opinion for
the cost of the judge calls alone, before spending anything on a fresh run.

This paid for itself immediately during a 528-rep run: the judge hit rate limits
twice, and both times the run was recovered from the saved transcripts at **zero
model cost**. Without saved transcripts, a rate limit three hours in means
starting over.

## Why this is a design stance, not a feature

There's a cheap version of an eval tool where every question costs a full re-run,
because a re-run is easy to implement and the tokens are the user's problem. It
also quietly discourages the thing you most want people doing: interrogating
their own results.

So the layering is deliberate, cheapest first:

| command | re-runs the agent? | re-runs the judge? | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rescore` | no | no | **free** |
| `grade` | no | yes | judge only |
| `run` | yes | yes | full |

If a question can be answered from artifacts already on disk, answering it should
be free. Adjusting your ship bar and seeing what it does to six months of history
is a `rescore` away, not a budget request.
